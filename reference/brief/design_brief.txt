UPPER SCOOP
Build a forward-facing hollow raised scoop. Smooth entrance lip, starting internal radius about 3 mm. Inner roof approximately 63 above local hood from Y=-65 through Y=+40, keeping the full downward passage over the aperture, whose aft edge is Y=+35.5. Smoothly close the tail by Y=+95; expose tail length as a parameter so it can be extended if room and printing allow. This short tail is a packaging choice, not an optimized low-drag aerodynamic profile. No rear-facing opening. No obstruction or decorative web across the throat.
The hood may form the floor outside the hole if the perimeter seal is continuous. Fit the flange to a smooth representation of the actual local outer hood curvature, or use a replaceable contoured adapter and gasket. Do not force a flat warped part down with bolt torque. Do not reuse the old hood curvature polynomial from a different location. Avoid converting the full scan mesh into thousands of CAD faces.

LOWER AIR GUIDE — DEFAULT OPEN CONFIGURATION
Use a removable broad chute/deflector connected to the underside mounting assembly. It moves with the hood. Its inlet follows the rounded 195 x 71 opening; use a radiused entry and no smaller hose neck. Start with two side cheeks and a smooth turning wall that redirects downward flow toward the exposed filter media. The outlet and underside remain open where needed for clearance, engine movement and drainage. Keep clear of the cone's solid end cap and avoid pressing against the filter.
Guide depth, guide reach, guide yaw, turn angle, outlet width, outlet height, and engine/filter clearances are physical-fit parameters, NOT dimensions inferred from the nozzle mouth. If the scan cannot establish them, create an adjustable/shallow fit-check version and a hinge/slot or interchangeable guide geometry, explicitly labeled provisional. Do not invent a full nozzle depth from the 71 mm mouth length.
Allow a 25-50 mm outlet-to-filter gap as a starting packaging target only where measured motion permits. Check all hood sweep positions and engine movement. Use broad radii and no unnecessary corrugated hose. Air can spill from this open guide; it is a practical supplemental cooling feed, not a pressure-sealed intake.

OPTIONAL LATER CLOSED CONFIGURATION
Provide an optional enclosed wide duct or airbox interface with the same hood opening and fastener pattern. Target a minimum clear cross-sectional area of 100-110 cm2; prefer about 120-135 cm2 where packaging allows. Evaluate areas in sections normal to the local flow path, not just plan-view widths. The rounded 195 x 71 cut is approximately 137.6 cm2; 200 x 60 mouth is 120 cm2 before mesh. If adding mesh, keep high open area and adjust dimensions so it does not become the smallest restrictive section; record open-area assumptions. Do not reduce the system to a 63.5 mm bore (~31.7 cm2). A later sealed airbox requires pressure/temperature testing; these geometry targets do not prove sufficient pressure recovery or flow.

HOOD FASTENING AND BACKING
Use eight M5 through-bolts, metal washers/clamp strips, metal compression limiters, positive locking nuts suitable for actual temperature, and a conforming metal underside load-spreading frame or strips. Initial local hole centers:
(-115,-75), (+115,-75), (-115,0), (+115,0), (-115,+105), (+115,+105), (0,-75), (0,+105).
Positions are parameters, to be moved if ribs/tool clearance require. Keep appropriate edge distance and distributed retention. Initial nominal hole-center distance from outer flange edge is 10 mm. Show washer diameters and tool-access envelopes. Put mounting bolts outside the airflow chamber.
Stack: bolt head -> metal washer/clamp strip -> printed flange with compression limiter -> weather gasket -> hood sheet -> metal backing -> washer -> locking nut.
A compression limiter must bear on metal across the relevant compressible stack; a sleeve floating inside plastic does not solve creep. Derive sleeve and bolt lengths from measured installed stack and specified gasket compression. Do not assign a generic tightening torque to crush thin sheet or plastic. Do not pull two separated hood skins together with bolt preload: move the hole or provide fitted spacers across a measured gap. Do not drill/cut reinforcement without a separate assessment; prefer keeping the owner's hole location and adapting flange/backing details.
All primary scoop retention must be through-bolted and metal-backed. No reliance on self-tappers, adhesive tape, printed screw threads, or heat-set inserts alone. The lower guide attaches independently to metal-backed captive hardware and can be removed without dismantling the upper scoop. Keep retention hardware captive or outside the airflow; nothing loose above the cone. Provide access for assembly and maintenance.

HEAT, PRINTING, WATER
ASA is a candidate for the exterior scoop, subject to the exact filament datasheet, actual surface temperature and print quality. Do not treat a published softening point as allowable sustained clamped temperature. Under-hood guide material needs its own heat assessment; allow a formed-metal guide using the same mounts if measured conditions exceed the printed material's capability. No unqualified approval of PLA. Printed parts need generous fillets, local flange gussets, sufficient perimeters and solid fastener zones; specify actual slicer wall results. Check warping, support removal and the printer's usable build area for a 250 mm-wide flange. If splitting, use a mechanically supported joint away from critical throat/fastener regions, not a thin glued butt seam.
Include an installed-low-point drainage path away from filter and electrical parts; keep rain from being deliberately dumped onto the cone. Offer a removable broad splash deflector if needed, without choking flow. The scoop is not rain-proof. Preserve the proper engine air filter.
